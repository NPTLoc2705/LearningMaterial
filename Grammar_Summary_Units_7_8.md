## PART 1. UNIT 7: COOL APPS AND GADGETS

### 1. Superlative adjectives (Lesson 2)

Use a superlative to say that one person or thing has the most (or least) of a quality in a group.

| Adjective type | Rule | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 syllable | the + adj + **-est** | long → the longest · tall → the tallest |
| 1 syllable, consonant + vowel + consonant | Double the last consonant, then add **-est** | big → the biggest |
| 1 syllable, ends in **-e** | Add **-st** | large → the largest |
| 2 syllables, ends in **-y** | Change **-y** to **-iest** | easy → the easiest |
| 2 syllables (other) | the + **most / least** + adj | honest → the most / least honest |
| 3 or more syllables | the + **most / least** + adj | exciting → the most / least exciting |
| Irregular | good → the **best** · bad → the **worst** | the best feature · the worst singer |

Examples from the lesson:

- I am the tallest in my family.
- This box is the biggest one.
- My house is the largest one in our neighborhood.
- This exam is the easiest one.
- This is the most famous dish.
- It is the most interesting movie in my life.

### 2. Future: *will* vs. *going to* (Lesson 4)

|  | will + V | going to + V |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decisions / plans | **Instant decisions** (decided while speaking). *I will buy some milk after work.* | **Plans / intentions** (already decided before speaking). *I'm going to buy some milk after work.* |
| Predictions | **Opinion** (we think something will happen). *They won't win their next match.* | **Based on evidence** (we can see it now). *Look at those clouds. It's going to rain.* |

Useful pattern for opinion + decision: **I think I'll …** / **I don't think I'll …** + verb

- It's cold today. I don't think I'll go out.
- I'm hungry. I think I'll have something to eat.

### 3. Making and responding to requests (Lesson 1, Speaking)

| Making requests | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Could / May I + V …? | Could I borrow your pen? |
| Can you + V …? | Can you lend me your smartphone? |
| Please let me + V | Please let me go out. |

| Responding to requests | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Of course. Here you are! / Sure. Here you go. | Agree |
| Sorry. / I'm sorry. | Refuse |

### 4. Word-level language points in Unit 7

- **Suffix -ible / -able** (Lesson 1): both make adjectives. *-ible* cannot stand alone (possible – poss); *-able* can stand alone (suitable – suit).
- **Parts of speech** (Lesson 2): noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, preposition.
- **Adjectives in a product review** (Lesson 5): use adjectives to help the reader understand your opinion.
- Lesson 3 (video lesson) has no new grammar target.

## PART 2. UNIT 8: INTO THE PAST

### 1. Present perfect (Lesson 2)

**Use:** to describe an action that began in the past and continues in the present. Lesson 3 also practices *Have you ever …?* (life experience) as a warm-up.

| Form | Structure | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative (+) | S + have / has + V past participle | They have learned English for 2 years. |
| Negative (−) | S + have / has + not + V past participle | They have not met each other since 1999. |
| Question (?) | Have / Has + S + V past participle? | Have you completed the final exam? |

More examples from the activities:

- People have played cards for about twelve hundred years.
- Players have created about one hundred different solitaire games.
- Many players haven't played solitaire with cards since computers became popular.
- How often have you played it? · How many times have you won?
- Warm-up: Have you ever eaten sushi? → Yes, I have.

### 2. *For* and *since*

| Word | Use | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **for** + period of time | Shows the **amount of time** | We have talked for 30 minutes in the class. · It has rained for a week. |
| **since** + starting point | Shows **when** an event started | I have waited here since 9 a.m. · My parents have lived in Vietnam since 1980. |

### 3. Cause and effect (Lesson 2, Reading strategy)

| Word | Introduces | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **because** | Cause | She is never interested in studying because she wants to become a basketball player. |
| **since** | Cause (same meaning as *because*) | I don't think he wants to attend the party since he is an introvert. |
| **so** | Effect | It rained, so I got wet. |
| **As a result,** | Effect | She used to play taekwondo. As a result, she knows how to defend herself. |

Note: *since* has two meanings. With a time (since 1980) it is a starting point; with a clause (since he is an introvert) it means *because*.

### 4. *There* + *to be* (Lesson 4)

Use *there* + *to be* to say that something exists or happens. The verb agrees with the noun that follows.

| Tense | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present | There is / There are | There are twenty chairs in the classroom. |
| Past | There was / There were | There were three worms in my salad. |
| Present perfect | There has been / There have been | There have been many earthquakes in this century. |
| Future (will) | There will be | There will be an excellent party next weekend. |
| Future (going to) | There is / are going to be | There are going to be fireworks at the festival. |

|  | Positive | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Present | There is / are | There isn't / aren't | Is / Are there …? |
| Past | There was / were | There wasn't / weren't | Was / Were there …? |
| Future | There will be | There won't be | Will there be …? |

### 5. WH-questions (Lesson 4, Extra grammar)

WH-questions ask for specific information. The answer is not just *yes* or *no*.

| WH-word | Asks about | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What** | Things, animals, objects | What is this? – It's a book. |
| **Where** | Place | Where do you live? – I live in Hanoi. |
| **When** | Time | When do you go to school? – In the morning. |
| **Why** | Reason | Why do you like to eat apples? – Because they are sweet. |
| **Who** | People (subject) | Who do you love the most? – My mom. |
| **Whom** | People (object of the verb or preposition) | With whom am I speaking? – You are speaking with Lisa. |
| **Which** | Making a choice | Which color do you prefer to wear? – Pink. |
| **How** | Manner, condition, quality; also distance, length, quantity, age | How old are you? – I'm 11 years old. |

The lesson practices turning a statement into a question in different tenses:

- What is she doing in her room? (present continuous)
- When do they play soccer? (present simple)
- What did Tom buy last week? (past simple)
- When will the concert start? (future with *will*)
- Where are we going this weekend? (*going to* / present continuous)
- How long has he been working at a software company? (present perfect continuous)

### 6. Talking about likes and dislikes (Lesson 1, Speaking)

| Likes | Dislikes |
| --- | --- |
| I am (really) into … | I don't like … |
| I like … / I love … | I'm not into … |
| … is my favorite. | I dislike / hate … |
| I don't mind … / … doesn't bother me. (neutral) |  |

Examples: Eating healthy food is my favorite. · I don't like staying up late.

After these expressions, the lesson examples use a noun or a verb + **-ing**.

### 7. Other language points in Unit 8

- **Suffix -ful** (Lesson 1): noun + -ful → adjective meaning "full of" or "characteristic of" (help → helpful, wonder → wonderful, thank → thankful, joy → joyful, peace → peaceful).
- **Contrast connectors in the writing model** (Lesson 5): *Unlike …, Instead, However, both … and …*, used to compare how different places celebrate the summer solstice. The model also uses *There have been … since ancient times* (present perfect).
- Lesson 3 (video lesson) has no new grammar target beyond the *Have you ever …?* warm-up.
